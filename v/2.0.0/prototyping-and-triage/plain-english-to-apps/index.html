Module 2 · Natural-language prototyping and requirement analysis

From plain English to a working prototype

Describe an app in plain language and get something people can click. Then decide what the prototype is for, and what it isn't, before it reaches engineering.

👥 For: Non-technical⏱ ~45 minGraded · 4 pt

Why this matters#

A clickable prototype answers questions that a document can't: "Is this the flow we meant?", "Which fields are actually needed?", "Will customers understand this screen?" With the Codex app, a PM, BA or designer can build one in an afternoon by describing it. There's no hand-off and no waiting for a sprint.

The risk is the opposite one. A prototype that looks finished gets treated as if it were finished. This topic covers both: how to get a good prototype, and how to keep it in its place.

Describe the result, not the code#

You don't need technical words. You need the same things a good brief has: who it's for, what they do, which data it shows, and how you'll know it works.

Asking for a prototype
✗ Vague
Make an app for leave requests.

Codex has to invent the users, the fields, the rules and the look, and you'll spend the afternoon correcting guesses.

✓ Specific
Build a clickable prototype of a leave-request tool for a 40-person office.
Users: employees submit requests; team leads approve or reject them.
Screens: "My requests" list, "New request" form (dates, type, comment),
lead's inbox with Approve / Reject and a required reason for rejecting.
Use the sample data in leave_sample.csv. No real logins or emails.
Done when: I can submit a request as an employee, switch to the lead view,
reject it with a reason, and see the status change in "My requests".

Users, screens, data, boundaries ("no real logins") and a walkthrough that proves it works.

Let Codex interview you

Not sure what to specify? Start with "Before building anything, ask me up to 8 questions about users, screens and data." The questions it asks are a free gap analysis of your requirements.

What a prototype is, and what it isn't#

A prototype is A prototype is not
A way to agree on flow, fields and wording Production code engineering can "just ship"
Something to put in front of users this week Proof that the data model, security or performance work
A sharper requirement: "like this, but…" A commitment on scope or a date
Use case
Prototype before the estimate
Business analyst

Situation

The client describes a "simple" onboarding portal. The team suspects it's not simple, but there is no spec to estimate.

Prompt

Build a clickable prototype of the onboarding portal described in client_notes.md.
Use fake data only. After it works, list:
1) every assumption you made that isn't in the notes,
2) every screen or rule where the notes were ambiguous,
3) the questions I should ask the client before engineering estimates this.

Good outcome

A prototype for the client call, plus a list of assumptions and open questions that becomes the agenda.

Tips

  • The list of assumptions is often worth more than the prototype.
  • Label it clearly as a prototype in the shared link and on the first screen.
