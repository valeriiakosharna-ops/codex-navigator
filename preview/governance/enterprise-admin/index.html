Module 5 · Governance, safety and enterprise rollout

Enterprise administration

How a workspace rolls Codex out, step by step. Owners, identity and roles, enforced runtime requirements, plugin controls, analytics, the Compliance API and the HIPAA setup, plus what each control can and can't prove.

For technical specialists⏱ ~50 minGraded · 5 pt

Why this matters#

For an organisation, "using Codex" means several access boundaries at once: who can sign in, what the agent may do on a laptop, what it may do in the cloud, which plugins and company systems it can reach, and what gets recorded. Each one is set in a different place. A rollout goes smoothly when someone owns each boundary and every change is tested.

This topic is for technical specialists who plan or run a workspace rollout. You won't configure anything here. The goal is to know the map well enough to plan a rollout and ask the right questions.

Enforced settings: requirements and defaults#

OpenAI Docs
Managed configuration
Officialdocs⏱ 15 minadvanced

How admins set requirements (enforced, so users can't loosen them) and defaults (starting values users may change) for sandbox, approvals, network, MCP servers, plugins and hooks, from the workspace or on the device.

While you study, pay attention to

  • Which settings belong in requirements (anything that protects the company) and which are fine as defaults (comfort settings).
  • How managed settings reach local clients, and how to verify they took effect.

The two-test habit

OpenAI's security cookbook recommends that after every configuration change you test one workflow that should work and one that should be blocked. Most rollout incidents are a control that was "configured" but never actually blocked anything.

Regulated data: HIPAA#

OpenAI Docs
HIPAA configuration guide for Codex
Officialdocs⏱ 20 minadvanced

For organisations handling protected health information, it sets out the shared responsibility. OpenAI covers its services under a BAA. You configure workstations, repositories, local retention, local MCP servers and plugins.

While you study, pay attention to

  • Local TOML policy files govern what Codex can do on a user's machine. That makes them part of your HIPAA safeguards.
  • ChatGPT sign-in vs API-key sign-in follow different retention and permission rules.
  • The guide is explicit that it isn't legal advice. Review the final configuration against your own policies.
Open the material
Even if you aren't in healthcare

The HIPAA guide is the most complete worked example of "regulated data meets coding agent". Its structure (shared responsibility, then role-based access, then reviewed plugins, then enforced requirements, then a staged rollout) works well for GDPR-sensitive or client-confidential projects too.
