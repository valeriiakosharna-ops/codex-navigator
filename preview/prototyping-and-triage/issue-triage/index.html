Module 2 · Natural-language prototyping and requirement analysis

Issue triage and human-reviewed estimates

Let Codex sweep the backlog, group duplicates, suggest priorities and flag missing information, then turn its estimates into proposals the team reviews.

For non-technical specialists⏱ ~45 minGraded · 4 pt

Why this matters#

Triage is the part of delivery that everyone needs and nobody enjoys. Reports come in from support, Slack threads, alerts and failed checks. Half of them are duplicates and the other half lack the one detail an engineer needs. Codex is good at the sweep: reading everything, grouping it, and pointing out what's missing.

It isn't the one who decides. Priorities, commitments and estimates affect people and contracts, so they stay proposals until a person on the team has reviewed them.

A triage report you can trust#

The official recipe asks for a report in which every item has the same parts. Use it as your quality bar:

Each item has Why
Priority (P0–P3) A suggestion you can accept or change
Evidence: links or short quotes So you can check it in seconds
Facts and guesses kept apart So a guess never reaches the backlog dressed up as a fact
Recommended next action So the report leads to a decision, not a discussion
Duplicates grouped under one item So three reports of one bug don't look like three bugs
Asking for triage
✗ Vague
Go through the backlog and prioritise everything. Update the tickets.

Codex is allowed to change shared tickets, there's no evidence requirement, and guesses look the same as facts.

✓ Specific
Triage issues opened in [project] in the last 7 days.
For each issue: suggested priority P0–P3, a category, evidence (link or quote),
missing information an engineer would need, and dependencies on other issues.
Group duplicates. Mark anything you infer as "assumption".
Do not edit, label, assign or close anything. Return a report only.

Read-only, evidence-based, with assumptions marked. A person decides what changes in the tracker.

Estimates: provisional, with assumptions#

An estimate from Codex is useful as a first draft that shows its reasoning. It's dangerous as a number on its own. Ask for ranges and assumptions, and let the people doing the work adjust it.

Use case
First-pass estimates for a refinement session
Project manager

Situation

Twenty new backlog items need rough sizes before Thursday's refinement. Engineers have no time to read them all in advance.

Prompt

For each item in this list, give a provisional size (S / M / L / XL) and:
- the assumptions behind it;
- what information is missing that could change the size;
- dependencies on other items or teams;
- your confidence (low / medium / high).
Don't present any size as a commitment. Sort the items with low confidence first.

Good outcome

A refinement agenda that starts with the items most likely to be wrong, with questions ready for the engineers.

Tips

  • The low-confidence items are where the team's time goes. The draft tells you where to look.
  • Record which sizes the team changed and why. That feedback improves the next prompt.
Decisions that stay human

Commitments to clients, priority calls that affect someone's work, closing or rejecting a customer's report, and anything about people's performance. Codex can prepare these. A person on the team makes them and owns them.
